# Car & Motorcycle Short-Form Scripting Brief

Reference material for a script-writing agent. Platforms: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts. Mixed on-camera and faceless voiceover.

**Evidence tiers used throughout.** `[V]` = re-verified against live channel data sorted by views, 31 Jul 2026. `[O]` = single-pass observation (recent-uploads window, transcript, or frame read) — directionally useful, not a performance profile. `[U]` = untested hypothesis; the community-sentiment leg of the research was second-hand and could not be opened. Never present `[U]` material to a user as a finding.

**Two corpus-level corrections that override older guidance:**

1. **Instruction is the dominant winning format in automotive short-form, not the loser.** An earlier draft of this brief said "how-to" framing reliably underperforms. That came from sampling recent-uploads windows and is backwards. Top Shorts on the exact channels cited: MotoJitsu "Quick Lesson About The Clutch" 6.3M, "How To Swerve On A Motorcycle" 2.5M; ChrisFix "How to Remove Locking Lug-Bolts without the Key" 6M; Adobo Moto's top eight Shorts are all how-to installs (chain install 1.3M, 600-mile service 1.3M); The Car Care Nut "How To Make Your Toyota Last Over 300,000 Miles Part 1" 1.4M / "Part 3" 1.1M; Chevy Dude Shorts "How To Ask A Car Dealer To Lower Their Fees" 1.3M, channel top Short 14M. `[V]`
2. **What actually fails is the templated multi-clause course-catalogue title**, e.g. "Mastering Motorcycle Braking_ Achieve Balance and Control in Your Stops" (note the literal underscore from a repurposing template). The same channel's best performer is "How much lean angle before you lose traction on your motorcycle?" — instruction phrased as a physical question with an answer. `[V]`

## 1. Niche map

The organizing insight: **every automotive tribe polices the same sin — appearance outrunning function — but each defines "function" differently.** That one frame generates all the contempt. Stance is the deliberate exception, which is why it draws the most cross-tribe hostility.

Pick **one** tribe per script and stay inside its frame. A script praising a stanced car for its lap times was written by someone who read about both and understood neither.

### Car-side

| Tribe | Respects | Holds in contempt | Insider vocabulary (use sparingly, correctly) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **JDM / tuner** | Chassis-code and engine-code literacy, swaps done properly, tuning with datalogs | Cosmetic mods imitating speed; non-functional wings, scoops, fart-can exhausts | Chassis codes (AE86, EK9, S13/S14/S15, FD3S, JZA80), engine codes (2JZ-GTE, RB26DETT, 4G63, K24, 13B-REW), "single turbo," "built vs boosted," "TRA-Kyoto." **Avoid "ricer"** — dated and racially loaded; using it casually marks the speaker as out of touch |
| **Stance / fitment** | Fitment precision, camber/offset math, event pedigree (Wekfest, StanceNation, Slammedenuff), wheel specs | "Function-only" people as joyless; poke/stretch done badly | Offset/ET, poke, stretch, tucking, air-over-static, "hellaflush" is dated. **Note: this tribe inverts the function test on purpose. Most archetypes in this brief are hostile to it — see §8** |
| **Track / time-attack** | Seat time, data, consistency, brake and tire management | Horsepower with no lap times; "mall dyno" numbers | Apex, trail braking, brake bias, tire temps/pressures hot vs cold, sector times, "slow in, fast out" is a cliché to them |
| **Classic / restoration** | Numbers-matching, correct date codes, hand fabrication | Trailer queens; restomod purists vs originality purists fight internally | Numbers-matching, date-coded, survivor, barn find, patina (contested — see below), concours, driver-quality |
| **Truck / overland** | Actual trail use, recovery competence, knowing your own build spec | Unused capability. Verbatim community tells: "nerf bars instead of real sliders," "sliders but never use them," "more lip than sidewall," "a lift with 20-inch rims and it's 2wd," and the kill shot — not knowing your own lift: "Not really sure, I took it into the shop and it looks rad" | Rock sliders vs nerf bars, recovery points vs tie-downs, spacer lift vs proper lift, departure angle, aired-down, mall crawler |
| **Euro / VAG** | Servicing knowledge, timing chain/carbon build-up literacy, chassis balance | Badge inflation (M badge on a 328i), leased-and-tuned | **Bimmer = BMW car, Beemer = BMW motorcycle.** Chassis codes (E30, E46, F80, B8, MK7), "carbon clean," DSG vs DCT |
| **American muscle** | Cubic inches, drag times, drivetrain durability | Cosmetic muscle; "Mustang leaving cars and coffee" jokes are exhausted | 60-foot time, ET, trap speed, cam specs, "LS/LT swap," "coyote," "solid roller" |
| **EV / tech** | Instrumented data, charge-curve behavior, efficiency in real conditions | Vibes-based range claims; both fanboy and hater posturing | kWh/100mi or mi/kWh, C-rate, taper, preconditioning, SoC, DCFC, one-pedal, 800V architecture, LFP vs NMC |
| **Exotic / lifestyle** | Access, rarity knowledge, actually driving the thing | Implied ownership of rented or borrowed cars; "I can't afford my house but I'm keeping the hypercar" as a bit |
| **Consumer buyer** | Plain numbers, dealer-process specifics, money saved | Enthusiast gatekeeping aimed at them. **This is a different audience — see §8.8** | Out-the-door price, doc fee, dealer add-ons, money factor, residual, buy rate vs marked-up rate |

### Moto-side

| Tribe | Respects | Holds in contempt | Vocabulary (do not mix registers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sportbike** | Body position, corner entry, track days | Squids: inadequate gear *plus* speed *plus* showing off *plus* thin skill | Clip-ons, rearsets, trail braking, chicken strips (contested), knee-down, hanging off |
| **Cruiser / Harley** | Miles, wrenching, club/ride history | Metric-cruiser posturing; being lectured by sportbike riders. **Largest paying moto demographic** | Bagger, brain bucket, stage kit, "the wave," V-twin cadence |
| **ADV** | Actual mileage, self-recovery, load management | The bike that never leaves pavement — "the guy whose weekend goal is a good parking spot in front of Starbucks" | Farkle, panniers, tank slap, standing on the pegs, "the GS lean" |
| **Dirt / supermoto** | Bike control, jump technique | Street-only posers on knobbies | Roost, whiskey throttle, holeshot, scrub, dab |
| **Vintage / café** | Getting old machinery running and keeping it running | "A holocaust of 1970s crap bikes" is the affectionate insider joke about the scene itself | The ton, ton-up, clip-ons, points, jetting, kick-only |
| **Touring** | Comfort engineering, route knowledge, mileage | Speed-first content | Farkle also lives here, saddle time, wind management |

### Vocabulary discipline (hard rule)

These terms are **not one register**. *Farkle* is ADV/touring. *The ton* is British café-racer. *Brain bucket* is old-school cruiser. *Roost* is dirt. *Clip-ons/rearsets* is sportbike/café. **Three of them in one script is the loudest possible tourist signal — worse than using none.** Maximum one tribe-specific term per script unless the creator is demonstrably native to that tribe.

The shaming vocabulary — *squid, organ donor, meat crayon, squid math, dress for the slide not the ride* — is real and correctly documented, but it exists next to a live counter-reaction (*gear nazi, ATGATT nanny*). **The agent may not deploy it at a third party.** See §4.

**Patina is a tribal landmine.** Vice Grip Garage romanticizes it and it works for that audience. To a restoration or auction audience, patina is neglect or a defect. Route on the creator's tribe, not on the aesthetic.

## 2. Format archetypes that work

Each entry: **cold open → tension → payoff → runtime → who can run it.**

### A. Macro-object cold open `[O, strong]`
**Open:** a single failed part fills the frame on a fingertip. Shallow DOF, natural light, no music, no text. **Tension:** what did this do, and how did it get like that. **Payoff:** name it deadpan at ~0.9s, then the mechanism. **Runtime:** 20–35s. **Faceless-safe — actually hands-only, which is the point: hands are the cheapest credential and they defeat every gatekeeping attack.**
Evidence: Watch Wes Work, "The Mystery of the Over Torqued Lug Nuts," 1.2M views for a photograph of a lug nut. Speech begins at 0.87s: *"this is an over-torqued lug nut."*
**Write shot one as "macro on [specific part], shallow DOF" before writing anything wider.** One object beats one scene.

### B. Obscure-part explainer `[V — cleanest single result in the corpus]`
**Open:** the part in the hand, named, with a price. **Tension:** you have never seen this and it solves a problem you have. **Payoff:** mechanism at 5–8s, relevance to a normal vehicle at the end. **Runtime:** 30–45s. **On-camera or hands-only.**
Evidence: Papadakis Racing (Formula Drift team, 638K) — "Trick NASCAR remote oil filter adapter explanation" **430K vs 22K for the next-best Short on the channel**. On an enthusiast channel, an obscure component explained by someone who actually uses it beat the spectacle the channel exists for by ~20×.

### C. Short-imperative or answerable-question instruction `[V]`
**Open:** the task named in ≤6 words, or a physical question with a real answer. **Tension:** you are doing this wrong / you don't know the number. **Payoff:** demonstration begins by 3–5s. **Runtime:** 25–60s. **On-camera or hands-only; faceless only with original footage of the procedure.**
Title patterns that win: "How To Swerve On A Motorcycle," "How to Remove Locking Lug-Bolts without the Key," "How to install a motorcycle chain," "How To Ask A Car Dealer To Lower Their Fees," "How much lean angle before you lose traction?"
Title patterns that lose: multi-clause SEO constructions with colon-subtitles and a benefit list.
**If the script says "I torqued it to spec," the shot must include the wrench clicking.**

### D. Quoted-claim disproof (repair-order register) `[O, strong]`
**Open:** a scare-quoted third-party assertion as the title and overlay — `"Other Shop" Says You Need A Motor!`, `Customer States: Mount and Balance Tires`. **Tension:** someone is wrong and you have the vehicle. **Payoff:** the rebuttal evidence on screen inside 4s. **Runtime:** 30–50s. **On-camera or hands-only.**
Evidence: South Main Auto 2.5M. The literal repair-order voice is the whole title system, and it is unfakeable if you don't work in a shop.

### E. Walking evidence reveal `[O, strong]`
**Open:** unstabilized handheld vertical, VO poses a shared grievance as a question. **Tension:** ≤2s gap between question and proof. **Payoff:** the camera walk lands on the physical proof. **Runtime:** 30–45s. **On-camera/VO over own footage only.**
Evidence: South Main Auto 5.2M — question at 2.07s, transmission on the concrete by 3.7s. **A rhetorical question with a delayed payoff is the clickbait pattern that gets punished. Two seconds, maximum.**

### F. Failure post-mortem `[O, three independent channels]`
**Open:** the carnage on the bench, not the finished build. **Tension:** what killed it. **Payoff:** the actual cause by 6–10s, the fix after. **Runtime:** 30–60s. **On-camera, hands-only, or faceless-with-credited-footage.**
Evidence: Donut — "Learning why my Porsche engine failed so catastrophically" 744K, "Cutting my Porsche's engine apart" 1.7M, both above the assembly Shorts in the same arc. Also South Main, Hoovie. **Lead with the failure, not the win.** Admitting your own failure is the highest-trust move available.

### G. Receipts / real cost `[O]`
**Open:** the number that hurts, stated in the first 3s. **Tension:** it's worse than you think. **Payoff:** itemization — "$4,200, and $1,800 of that was one sensor." **Runtime:** 25–40s. **Requires the creator's own money.**
Evidence: Hoovie's Garage (1.66M). Hard rule: **never quote MSRP as if it were a purchase price.** Readers of the enthusiast press name that specific move as a trope they hate. Attach a qualifier so a number becomes an argument: "$22,000 **in 2004 money**" (ThatDudeinBlue, 617K).

### H. Roll-on pull with a spec title `[V — newly identified, previously missed]`
**Open:** named bike/car, stated speed band, engine. No hook, no jeopardy, no personality, no contradiction. **Tension:** none needed — the sound and the number are the product. **Payoff:** the pull itself, 3–8s in. **Runtime:** 15–30s. **Faceless-native. Infinitely repeatable series scaffold.**
Evidence: Chase on Two Wheels — "2025 Yamaha R3 | 40-80 Pull" 623K, "2023 Kawasaki ZX-4RR | 40-80 Pull" 390K, against a channel whose Shorts were previously written off as failed. This is one of the most repeatable formats in moto short-form and no prior draft of this brief contained it.

### I. Silent spec card (sound as payload, text as contract) `[O, strong]`
**Open:** a pinned 2–3 line text card, top third, high contrast on a solid bar, static for the entire opening. No narration at all. **Tension:** the card states a complete claim; the sound is the reward for unmuting. **Payoff event:** 4–8s. **Runtime:** 15–30s. **Faceless-native.**
Evidence: carwow — multiple Shorts in the 500K–1.4M range with **no auto-caption track at all**, i.e. zero speech; its 7.69M Short contains exactly four spoken words in fourteen seconds: *"3, 2, 1, go."*
Deliverable is the **card copy + the sound event**, nothing else. Claim in the upper third, numbers/countdown in the lower third, action clear in the middle.

### J. Pure engine-note clip `[O]`
**Open:** bonnet-cam or side-tracking accel run, two-word label naming the exact car, cut to the tach ~3s. No speech, no music. **Runtime:** 10–20s. **Faceless-native. Zero claims means zero attack surface** — good low-risk cadence filler.
Evidence: savagegeese "Lexus LFA Engine Sound" 114K. Only works on a vehicle with a known sonic reputation.

### K. Clip-as-evidence breakdown (the compliant crash format) `[V for scale, policy-grounded]`
**Open:** the incident runs once at speed. **Tension:** what was available to the rider/driver and when. **Payoff:** slowed replay with the read, on-screen markup. **Runtime:** 30–60s. **Faceless-friendly — this is the strongest faceless model, because the narrator owns the read, not the footage.**
Evidence: DanDanTheFireman 676K (top Shorts 2.8M, 1.5M); Dashcam Lessons 992K.
**Structural non-negotiable:** the educational context must live in the spoken audio and the on-screen markup, because YouTube requires that context to "appear in the images or audio of the video itself." A safety hashtag under a shock clip is worth nothing. **Cut before impact and before injury.** Credit the clip source on screen — it solves rights and the "he stole this" attack in one move.

### L. Judgment invitation `[V]` — **gated**
**Open:** footage of someone making a mistake. **Tension:** the audience gets to rule. **Payoff:** freeze/replay on the error at 2–4s, your read at 4–15s. **Runtime:** 25–40s.
Evidence: MotoJitsu — "What Did He Do Wrong?" and "Motor Officers Tied Together!" ~1.1M each, "What Not To Do" 362K, "Don't Be This Guy" 218K.
**Gate:** this format delegates the gatekeeping impulse to the audience instead of triggering it against you — but only if the creator passes the same test. MotoJitsu can run it because his channel is explicitly ATGATT and his slow-speed control is on camera. **A creator filmed in a hoodie cannot run this format at all; it inverts into a hypocrisy pile-on.** Gate on the creator's own on-camera gear and riding, not on the topic.

### M. Contrarian claim about a named object `[O, strong]`
**Open:** a specific, disputable assertion about a specific product, stated flat. **Tension:** you disagree. **Payoff:** the evidence, immediately. **Runtime:** 30–50s.
Evidence: FortNine (2.35M) title system — "The Worst Reputation in Motorcycling," "BMW is Drilling Holes in Brake Levers," "10 Things We Lost on Modern Motorcycles," "Forget COG — This is Why the R1300GSA Rides like Nothing Else."
**Never pose the fight as an open question and walk away.** Take a side, give the strongest version of the opposing case, explain the tiebreak. Note F9's register is sportbike/ADV-coded with open contempt for cruiser orthodoxy — **not portable to a bagger audience.**

### N. Year/trim-range targeting explainer `[O]`
**Open:** "If you have a 2012–2014 Toyota Camry or a 2015 to 2017 Camry…" **Tension:** you are being addressed personally. **Payoff:** the specific failure and the specific fix. **Runtime:** 30–50s. **On-camera or faceless with correct-generation footage.**
Evidence: The Car Care Nut (1.8M subs; Shorts 1.2M–2.8M). Hyper-specific ranges make owners feel named and create a legitimate correction surface that drives comments.

### O. Revive and drive `[O]`
**Open:** the state of the thing plus the duration of neglect and a disqualifying obstacle. **Tension:** "can we anyway." **Payoff:** it runs / it moves. **Runtime:** 30–60s.
Three-part frame: **duration of neglect + disqualifying obstacle + implied "can we anyway."** e.g. "sitting in a carport for 6 years… doesn't have a battery… we can."
**Caution:** the FortNine video previously cited for this (`kHq_T7BN0_8`, "Can I UNSEIZE this Suzuki?", 2.6M) is **137 seconds long — not a Short.** The frame transfers; the timing data derived from it does not.

### P. Letterboxed in-group ritual `[O]`
**Open:** 16:9 footage centered in 9:16 with black bars, text in the top bar formatted "How to [ritual only owners of X do]." **Payoff:** the joke is the whole asset. **Runtime:** 10–20s.
Evidence: Throttle House 7.7M — "How to wave to another G-Wagen owner." **Deliberately unpolished. Do not gate the hook on production polish**: a 5.2M-view Short is unstabilized handheld phone footage.

### Q. EV-native archetypes — **newly added; the sound-led formats are structurally unavailable here**

Every ceiling format above (silent spec card, engine note, drag countdown, "script 5–8 seconds of raw exhaust") assumes an ICE soundtrack. EVs remove the payload. Substitute one of these:

- **Instrumented run.** Screen or gauge as the hero shot: charge curve from 10–80%, kWh/100mi at a stated speed and temperature, taper onset point. The number replaces the noise. Title carries the conditions ("0°C, 150kW charger, LFP pack").
- **Cost-per-mile receipts.** Same structure as Archetype G, with the electricity bill instead of the invoice.
- **Physical teardown.** Pack module, motor rotor, contactor, busbar — Archetype B works identically and is arguably richer, because almost nobody has seen these parts in hand.
- **Software-behavior gripe.** A specific, reproducible OTA or UI failure, filmed. High comment density, no policy surface.
- **The silence as the subject.** Tire roar, wind noise at 70, one-pedal modulation, HVAC compressor whine — invert the sound rule: mic what you *can* hear.
- **Mechanism explainer.** Engineering Explained is functionally faceless at the hook level (4.22M subs; top Shorts 41M/23M/23M/22M/20M `[V]`) because the authority is the physics, which is checkable by anyone.

Never write EV content as "soul" polemic from either side. That argument is identity, not spreadsheets — it produces jokes, not retention.

### Formats to avoid, and why

| Format | Why |
|---|---|
| **Guess-the-car / guess-the-logo / guess-the-exhaust** | The template is recognized on sight and swiped. Best single-creator datapoint: @shetalkscars posted "I'll be impressed if you can guess what car this is…" for 2,554 views against 67,400 on a value-buying hook. `[O, n=1]` — treat as directional. ID hooks are only viable as **real puzzles** (partial taillight, ambiguous trim) or second-order ones ("real trim, replica, conversion, or upbadge?") |
| **AI-TTS narration over stock or scraped footage** | YouTube's July 2025 inauthentic-content policy makes image slideshows, templated storylines and scrolling-text formats ineligible for monetization; TikTok flags compilations and light re-uploads as unoriginal. **Faceless is fine. Templated-with-no-original-input is not.** Do not repeat the specific casualty figures that circulated with this policy — they were sourced from a non-authoritative aggregator and are unusable |
| **Crash compilation aggregation** | Largest raw audience in moto, but it is an aggregation business, not a creator business, and it is the category most exposed to reused-content enforcement plus licensing claims. Dash Cam Owners Australia (604K) nearly lost its channel to viral-licensing firms that signed its own submitters; appeals failed because submitter permission didn't cover platform publication |
| **Withheld-payoff serialization** | Nuance, not a ban. Serialization works when each part restates the goal in one clause and delivers a complete unit — Car Care Nut's "300,000 Miles Part 1" 1.4M and "Part 3" 1.1M, Donut's engine parts 450K/592K `[V]`. What fails is Pt.1 that ends *before* the payoff to force Pt.2 |
| **Sportbike stunt demos (stand-up wheelie, hands-free)** | Simultaneously a tribal liability (reads as squid to ADV/cruiser/touring) and a monetization liability — see §6 ad tiers. Seated wheelie is green; stand-up is yellow |
| **Copying a signature prop** | MotoJitsu's die-cast demo bike is instantly recognized as his. Steal the *principle* — use a prop to demonstrate an error you cannot safely film — not the prop |
| **Staged discovery** | Filming before the drain plug comes out, multiple angles of an "unfolding" accident, implausibly clean fluids. `[U]` as measured sentiment, but mechanically sound and cheap to avoid |

## 3. Hook craft for automotive

### The mute contract

The single most useful structural finding: **the mute problem is solved by a pinned text card, not by subtitles.** Multiple 500K–1.4M carwow Shorts have no speech at all, and in each the card is pinned from frame zero and never leaves — it is the entire verbal layer, not a transcript.

> **Muted, the card must state a complete claim. Unmuted, the sound must be the payoff.**

Write the card and the spoken line as **separate deliverables that each work alone.** Never write a card that is a transcript of the VO. The single deliberate exception is the reaction/breakdown format, where reading the banner aloud is the style.

Card craft: static and persistent through the opening, not animated in or swapped out. Claim upper third, numbers/countdown lower third, action clear in the middle. Stacked variants build line-by-line rather than replacing (format label → subject → verdict + numeric score).

### First-word timing budget `[O, partial]`

Measured first-spoken-word timestamps: 0.87s (Watch Wes Work), 1.51s (MotoJitsu), 1.67s (RevZilla). On-camera hosts run later, 1.1–2.2s.

- **Faceless/VO: start speaking by ~0.9–1.7s. No face means no grace period.**
- **On-camera: up to ~2.2s.**
- Past 2.5s you need an established brand carrying it.

*Correction: a 1.55s FortNine datapoint previously in this budget came from a 137-second long-form video and has been removed.*

### Openers

Winning on-camera openers begin mid-thought, on a conjunction or filler: *"and a lot of people say…"*, *"so this lady with her Nissan uh oil everywhere"*, *"well Fellers I got good news and bad news"*, *"well I wasn't going to make any more shorts but…"*. Do not clean this up — over-polished delivery reads as marketing.

**Critical distinction the agent must not blur:** a mid-sentence cold open is legitimate when it is the creator's own continuous take or genuine incidental documentary audio. **It is a fabrication instruction when the agent writes the sentence for a third party's voice.** Never script a third party's line and cut into it as if captured.

The only creator observed opening with a self-introduction is ChrisFix — "Hey guys, Chris Fix here" at 2.15–2.24s, across Shorts from 2.3M to 27M. He can pay the branding tax because the brand *is* the promise. **Treat as an anti-pattern for anyone else.**

### Numbers that stop the scroll

Attach a qualifier so a number becomes an argument, not a stat:

- "$22,000 **in 2004 money**" > "$22,000"
- "sitting for **6 years**, and it doesn't even have a battery" > "sitting for 6 years"
- "**235,000 miles**" > "high mileage"
- A **/10 score** > any adjective
- Superlative + spec: "the largest brakes ever fitted to a production car"
- Scarcity finality in three clauses: "$3,500,000. Only 29 in the world. Sold out." — **note this is an exotic-tier device. Transplanted onto a $45K crossover it reads as a dealership ad**

### Title vs overlay division of labor

They must carry **different** information. Observed: title = "He Won't make it far" (outcome tease); overlay = "I RAN OVER HIS CHAIN" (cause). Duplicating them wastes the second-best piece of real estate you own.

### Question-to-proof gap

If the hook is a rhetorical question, **the physical proof must be on screen within 2 seconds.** Measured: question at 2.07s, evidence at 3.7s, 5.2M views. Never ask a question the audience cannot answer from the frame — "What did he do wrong?" over footage of the mistake is native; "What's the best sports car?" is marketing.

### Visual pattern-interrupts observed in-frame

Macro object on a fingertip → macro-to-wide reveal → hood/panel opening as the entire first 3s (wordless) → walking-camera evidence reveal → mid-sentence walk-in → prop demonstration → reaction split-frame (clip top two-thirds, presenter bottom third, banner at very top).

### Known-disputed source

One carwow video ID (`J8HXDSMTC3E`, ~1.44M) was cited in the research under two incompatible readings — as a wordless meme-caption gag and as an indicator-rating series entry. Both formats are real and both are described above; **do not attribute either to a specific ID in user-facing output.** Similarly, `a8bg_ES2jN8` (Throttle House, 3.75M) is **not** a Rolls-Royce walkaround — it is a one-off hot rod with a 27-litre Rolls-Royce **Merlin aero engine**. The "it's a Rolls-Royce, except it's kind of not" line describes a one-of-one freak. Generalizing it into an "identify-then-contradict" rule for ordinary cars produces exactly the treating-a-base-model-as-exotic cringe this brief bans.

## 4. Authenticity rules

These are hard constraints. A single violation invalidates a whole script in the comments.

### Terminology traps

- Inline-four is **never** a V4.
- Turbos are exhaust-driven; superchargers are crank/belt-driven.
- WRX is the car; WRC is the series.
- **Bimmer = BMW car. Beemer = BMW motorcycle.**
- Porsche is two syllables.
- A 2JZ is not a 1JZ and neither is an RB. Getting the engine on screen wrong under the narration is the canonical faceless death, and it is caught in minutes.

### Register lock (mandatory field before writing)

The research corpus mixes UK, Canadian, US and Australian creators freely. An unlocked agent will write "loonies" and "the bonnet" into a US script. **Lock market before line one:**

| | US | UK | CA | AU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Panel | hood / trunk | bonnet / boot | hood / trunk | bonnet / boot |
| Rubber | tire | tyre | tire | tyre |
| Currency | $ USD | £ | $ CAD | $ AUD |
| Fuel | gas, mpg (US) | petrol, mpg (imp) | gas, L/100km | petrol, L/100km |
| Fitment | aftermarket | aftermarket | — | — |

Never mix. Never use a regional in-joke ("loonies") outside its market.

### The catastrophizing ceiling — anti-Scotty-Kilmer rule

The corpus contains a jeopardy-framing rule ("front-load cost or a wrong done to someone"), and applied naively to routine maintenance it generates *"your cabin air filter is DESTROYING your engine."* **That register is the most-mocked voice in the vertical**, and ChrisFix is explicitly the trusted counter-example to it.

> **Jeopardy language is licensed only when the failure mode is real, named, and mechanism-explained.** If you cannot state the specific part, the specific failure, and the specific consequence, you may not use catastrophic verbs.

The paired rule: **no hyperbole about ordinary cars.** "This thing is an absolute WEAPON" about a mainstream sedan trim reads as a dealership ad. Superlatives require a superlative object.

### Provenance ladder (must be resolved before scripting)

Owned → borrowed from a friend → press fleet → rented → someone else's entirely. This determines which archetypes are available (receipts and ownership-confession require your own money) **and** triggers disclosure obligations (§6). Never imply ownership you don't have.

### The faceless-credibility problem

The wrong rule, which an earlier draft contained: *"write faceless scripts in first person with a stake — 'I bought this', 'this happened to me'."* Over footage the narrator did not shoot, **that is an instruction to fabricate.**

> **The rule: never write a sensory or first-person claim that the footage on screen cannot substantiate.** Write *around* footage you didn't shoot ("a stock 2JZ makes X — here's why the head gasket goes first"), never *inside* it. Credit third-party clips on screen. Where a claim needs presence, either get hands in frame or change the claim.

**Tells the audience actually catches, frame by frame:**
- Narration describes a sound the clip does not contain, or a music bed sits under "listen to that lifter tick."
- B-roll is the wrong generation, facelift, trim, or market — RHD footage under a US story, pre-facelift bumper under a facelift claim, wrong wheel, European plate.
- The engine on screen isn't the engine in the script.
- Mirrored/flipped stock footage, which puts the shifter, exhaust, or timing side on the wrong side.
- Multiple angles of a discovery that is supposedly unfolding live.
- Any first-person claim with no hands, no shop, no continuity.

**Four faceless models that survive**, all of which relocate authority away from ownership:
1. **Analysis over credited clips** — you own the read, not the footage, and you say so on screen.
2. **Documented / archival framing** — auction results, recall filings, NHTSA complaint data, EPA figures, period press. Nobody expects presence.
3. **The object in your own hand** — strictly hands-only, not faceless, and that's the point.
4. **Mechanism / physics claims** — checkable, so no credential needed.

### Gear content, first person only

You may not gear-shame a third party. *"Gear nazi"* and *"ATGATT nanny"* are live pejoratives and the counter-reaction is as strong as the norm. The defensible position is first-person ("I wear it because I've slid") plus the pragmatic line — **some gear beats none.**

Two exceptions that raise the bar sharply:
- If a rider in your footage is visibly under-geared, **address it in the script or don't use the footage.** Silence reads as endorsement and derails the comment section off your actual point.
- **Escalate hard when a passenger or a child is in frame.** Community tolerance for personal risk is broad; tolerance for imposed risk is near zero. This is precisely where creator standing collapses.

## 5. Comment provocation, done natively

**The strongest verified finding here contradicts standard CTA advice.** Two measured cases:

- FortNine's explicit CTA "What would you rather buy and why? 👇" drew 532 comments — but the **top comment (453 likes) ignored the binary entirely and nitpicked the model name** ("Even if it's called INT there, interceptor just sounds better"), and the third (264 likes) named a bike he never mentioned.
- Rainman Ray's "What's the mileage on the odometer??" drew 414 comments — top comments **ignored the question and discussed a coworker's demeanor.**

> **A nitpickable detail and a character beat out-drive an explicit CTA question.**

**What to write instead of a CTA:**
- **Plant one genuinely nitpickable detail** — a badge, a model designation, a trim distinction, a naming quirk — and let it sit.
- **Year/trim-range targeting** creates a legitimate correction surface: owners outside the range will say so.
- **Price/loyalty binaries asked affectionately**, not to farm outrage: "HOW MUCH DID YOU PAY?" (404K), "HOLDEN OR FORD?" (140K) — Mighty Car Mods, 3.98M `[V]`.
- **A specific ask tied to the content**: "what would you have done?" after a judgment clip, "what's the part you'd never buy used?" after a parts video. Generic "follow for more car content" is the marketing tell the audience is scanning for.

**Live arguments worth taking a side in** (state a position, give the strongest opposing case, explain the tiebreak): manual vs auto; EV driving character; loud pipes — **only when attached to a specific documented near-miss or a measured test**; ATGATT vs personal choice; stance vs function; restomod vs numbers-matching; overland gear vs actual trail use; "is X a real enthusiast car."

**Bait that gets punished** — mechanically predictable, though the sentiment evidence is `[U]`:
- Rider-vs-driver rage bait where the rider creates the hazard and the edit blames the motorist.
- Deliberate mechanical errors planted to farm corrections.
- Staged shop "discoveries."
- Obvious-answer ID posts.
- Exhausted brand jokes ("BMW drivers never signal," Subaru head gaskets, "the Supra is a BMW," VTEC jokes) — these produce jokes, not discussion, and they carry no argument. **Note: an earlier draft simultaneously banned "BMW drivers never signal" as a dead trigger and held up a carwow Short built on that exact joke as a ceiling format. Both claims were weakly sourced. Resolution: the joke is worn out for a cold account and available to a brand with a decade of equity behind it.**

**The equity gate, generalized:** wordless aesthetic B-roll and caption-only meme gags are **persona-dependent formats.** Vice Grip Garage's 9.7% like rate on a wordless clip (843K views / 82K likes) is a function of audience recognition, not of the shot. carwow can post a caption gag because it is an established marketplace brand with a known presenter. **On a cold account, the same footage needs a text card that states a claim, and the same caption is just a stale meme.**

## 6. Policy and risk constraints

### Never script

- **The creator performing an illegal maneuver on a public road.** No stand-up wheelies, no lane-splitting-at-speed hero shots, no top-speed runs, no street burnouts, no red-light runs.
- **A challenge or dare.** Meta removes "imagery depicting high-risk viral challenges, **regardless of context**" — there is no educational carve-out on Meta for this one. No "try this," no "tag someone who'd do this."
- **Speed as the payoff.** No "160 in a 55," no on-screen speedo callouts, no velocity-based titles. Posted footage is routinely the entire case file — a rider was charged after police reviewed 72 uploaded videos, served warrants on Google and Meta, and identified him because he showed his face in a Reel. Where the rider stayed helmeted and anonymous, the state declined: *"The account being owned by an individual is not enough to charge."*
- **A first-person admission of an offence on camera.** Insurers exclude racing/stunting and mine social media; the admission is both a charging aid and a coverage-denial aid.
- **Shock-bait titling on crash content.** YouTube names "Crazy accident" and "Warning: Lots of blood" as removal-triggering title patterns. Write the lesson or the question instead: "He had 1.2 seconds to react — watch what he did with them."
- **Emissions-defeat content.** EGR/DPF deletes, defeat tunes, catless downpipes. The EPA fines tuners and shops directly under the Clean Air Act.
- **Theft-adjacent instruction.** Lock-bypass, catalytic-converter removal, relay attacks. Note the boundary: a celebrated 6M-view Short is "How to Remove Locking Lug-Bolts without the Key" — legitimate owner-recovery content sitting one step from the line. Frame as owner-recovery on your own vehicle, or don't write it.

### Where the platform lines actually sit

**YouTube.** Removable: "Extremely dangerous driving: using a motor vehicle in a way that presents imminent risk of serious injury or death." Age-restriction test is three-part — educational/documentary/scientific/artistic context, the act does not risk serious injury, and the content does not promote the act. For violent or graphic content: *"For educational use of violent or graphic content, this context must appear in the images or audio of the video itself."*

**YouTube ad tiers — the actual granularity:**
| Tier | Includes |
|---|---|
| Full ads | Wheelies (seated) and ground-level parkour; motor vehicles speeding or drifting **without** dangerous tricks; accidents with only moderate visible impact (e.g. falling off a motorcycle); accidents with no exposed injury; **anything in a professional and controlled environment** |
| Limited ads | Speeding or drifting **with** dangerous tricks (stand-up wheelie, hands-free) or causing frequent disruptions; accidents with a strong enough moment of impact to cause upset |
| No ads | Shocking scenes and injuries — the moment of impact, someone unconscious |

**Meta.** Age-restricts to 18+ "imagery depicting dangerous forms of driving a motor vehicle that could lead to life-threatening injuries or death… except if shared in fictional or professional settings." An 18+ label on a Reel is functionally a reach kill — narrower than YouTube's carve-out.

**TikTok.** Content depicting activities likely to be imitated causing significant physical harm is removed; activities that may cause moderate harm are FYF-ineligible, 18+, and warning-labelled. Separately, **"moments leading up to a serious accident or injury, even if the injury itself isn't shown"** is 18+/FYF-ineligible — that is the near-miss format, named. FYF-ineligible videos stay findable via search and profile, and the flag is visible in analytics.

*Verification note: TikTok's Sept 2025 rewrite moved the granular driving bullets behind client-side expanders that could not be opened; treat pre-2025 driving-specific language as directional. Meta's recommendation-limitation claims rest on secondary reporting plus Community Standards text, not a primary Meta page.*

### The legal layer (absent from the source research entirely — flag all of these to the user)

1. **Defamation.** The "I got scammed by a [named dealership]" format is only available to someone with the paperwork. No named business without documentation. Frame as opinion, not factual assertion, wherever documentation is thin.
2. **Recording consent.** Two-party-consent states include FL, CA, IL, PA, WA, MA, MD. The marquee "cold-open on a third party's voice" example was filmed in Florida. **No identifiable third-party audio without consent.**
3. **Customer PII in shop content.** Plates, VINs, repair-order names, faces, addresses. Every garage-diagnosis archetype puts a customer's vehicle on camera.
4. **Disclosure is law, not tone.** FTC 16 CFR 255 (US) and ASA/CAP (UK): a loaned press vehicle, a comped part, or a covered trip is a **material connection requiring disclosure.**
5. **Press embargoes.** The first-look walkaround archetype runs on press-fleet access. Breaking an embargo means permanent OEM blacklisting — an existential risk to that whole format.
6. **Third-party footage rights.** Named licence source in the script metadata — ViralHog / Newsflare / Jukin licence ID, or a written grant that explicitly covers YouTube, TikTok and Instagram publication. Not Reddit rips, not "found on X."
7. **Music.** Default to original engine/ambient audio or a licensed library track (Epidemic, Artlist, Soundstripe). TikTok's catalogue licence does not travel to Meta or YouTube; business accounts are confined to the Commercial Music Library and Meta Sound Collection; any branded-content tag voids trending-audio use. On YouTube Shorts, licensed music shrinks the Creator Pool (1 track ≈ 50% to licensing, 2 ≈ 67%). **Additionally: incidental music at a cars-and-coffee or show routinely triggers Rights Manager and Content ID mutes** — "engine noise is the answer" does not cover ambient capture at events.
8. **"Made for kids" misflagging.** Bright vehicles, "cars" keywords and toy-adjacent thumbnails draw MFK flags, which kill comments and personalized ads.
9. **Reused content from the creator's own long-form.** A mechanical re-cut of your own long-form is treated differently from an original upload. If a Short is derived from long-form, it needs original narration, an original edit thesis, or new framing — not just a trim.
10. **Helmet-cam jurisdiction.** UK permits bracket-mounted helmet cams; France effectively bars public posting of helmet-cam footage; Austria and Portugal are stricter. Flag in the script brief.
11. **No cross-platform watermarks.** Produce clean masters, re-caption per platform.

## 8. Open questions — the agent must resolve these before writing a line

Unanswered, every one of these produces a specific, predictable failure.

1. **Which tribe is the core audience?**
   Car: JDM/tuner · stance/fitment · track · classic/restoration · truck/overland · Euro/VAG · American muscle · EV/tech · exotic/lifestyle · consumer-buyer.
   Moto: sportbike · cruiser/Harley · ADV · dirt/supermoto · touring · vintage/café.
   *Why it matters:* the archetype set is not tribe-neutral. **Stance is structurally excluded** by nearly everything in this brief — the dominant voices are all function-over-form, and stance inverts that test on purpose. If the audience is show/fitment, most archetypes here are hostile and the agent must say so rather than write them anyway. **Cruiser/Harley audiences reject the FortNine register.** **ADV, touring and cruiser read sportbike skill demos as squid.** **Consumer-buyer audiences are people buying a car, not enthusiasts** — applying engine-code literacy and "never say 'this car'" to them is a category error, and it is the audience attached to the 14M-view Short.

2. **Market register:** US / UK / CA / AU / EU. Currency, panel names, fuel measure, regulatory frame. Hard field, no default.

3. **ICE or EV** — because it decides whether sound is available as the payload. If EV, the sound-led archetypes are off the table and §2.Q applies.

4. **Does the creator personally pass this format's purity test?** Own gear on camera, own vehicle, own hands, own money. Specifically:
   - Judgment invitation requires visible ATGATT and visible competence.
   - Receipts requires the creator's own money.
   - Mall-crawler / poser taxonomy is a purity test the creator must survive — run it with a levelled truck on 20s in your own B-roll and you end yourself, and it insults the largest segment of the truck market.
   - Grievance formats require the paperwork.

5. **For each vehicle: owned, borrowed, press-fleet, rented, or someone else's?** Drives disclosure obligations and which archetypes are available.

6. **Is any footage third-party, and what is the licence?** Licence ID or written grant covering all three platforms, or the clip does not get used.

7. **Does the creator wrench themselves, and at what level?** Determines whether hands-in-frame is available — the cheapest credential there is.

8. **On-camera comfort, and existing audience equity.** Wordless B-roll, caption-only gags and self-introductions are all persona-dependent. A cold account gets none of them.

9. **Jurisdiction for any helmet-cam or in-car footage**, and whether any third-party audio was captured in a two-party-consent state.
