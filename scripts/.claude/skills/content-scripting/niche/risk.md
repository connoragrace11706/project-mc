# Policy, legal and risk constraints

Read before finalising anything involving speed, a maneuver, third-party
footage, a named business, or a person other than Sam.

## Never script

- **Sam performing an illegal maneuver on a public road.** No stand-up wheelies,
  no lane-splitting-at-speed hero shots, no top-speed runs, no street burnouts,
  no red-light runs.
- **A challenge or a dare.** Meta removes "imagery depicting high-risk viral
  challenges, **regardless of context**" — there is no educational carve-out on
  Meta for this one. No "try this," no "tag someone who'd do this."
- **Speed as the payoff.** No "160 in a 55," no on-screen speedo callouts, no
  velocity-based titles. **Posted footage is routinely the entire case file** —
  a rider was charged after police reviewed 72 uploaded videos and served
  warrants on Google and Meta, identifying him because he showed his face in a
  Reel. Where a rider stayed helmeted and anonymous, the state declined: *"The
  account being owned by an individual is not enough to charge."*
- **A first-person admission of an offence on camera.** Insurers exclude
  racing/stunting and mine social media; the admission is both a charging aid
  and a coverage-denial aid.
- **Shock-bait titling on crash content.** YouTube names "Crazy accident" and
  "Warning: Lots of blood" as removal-triggering title patterns. Write the
  lesson or the question instead: *"He had 1.2 seconds to react — watch what he
  did with them."*
- **Emissions-defeat content.** EGR/DPF deletes, defeat tunes, catless
  downpipes. The EPA fines tuners and shops directly under the Clean Air Act.
- **Theft-adjacent instruction.** Lock bypass, catalytic-converter removal, relay
  attacks. Note where the boundary sits: a 6M-view Short is "How to Remove
  Locking Lug-Bolts without the Key" — legitimate owner-recovery content one
  step from the line. **Frame as owner-recovery on your own vehicle, or don't
  write it.**

## Where the platform lines sit

**YouTube.** Removable: "Extremely dangerous driving: using a motor vehicle in a
way that presents imminent risk of serious injury or death." The age-restriction
test is three-part — educational/documentary/scientific/artistic context, the
act does not risk serious injury, and the content does not promote the act. For
violent or graphic content: *"For educational use of violent or graphic content,
this context must appear in the images or audio of the video itself."* [T1]

**YouTube ad tiers — the actual granularity:**

| Tier | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Full ads** | Seated wheelies and ground-level parkour; speeding or drifting **without** dangerous tricks; accidents with only moderate visible impact; accidents with no exposed injury; **anything in a professional, controlled environment** |
| **Limited ads** | Speeding or drifting **with** dangerous tricks (stand-up wheelie, hands-free) or causing frequent disruption; accidents with a strong enough moment of impact to cause upset |
| **No ads** | Shocking scenes and injuries — the moment of impact, someone unconscious |

**Meta.** Age-restricts to 18+ "imagery depicting dangerous forms of driving a
motor vehicle that could lead to life-threatening injuries or death… except if
shared in fictional or professional settings." **An 18+ label on a Reel is
functionally a reach kill** — narrower than YouTube's carve-out.

**TikTok.** Content depicting activities likely to be imitated causing
significant physical harm is removed; activities that may cause moderate harm
are FYF-ineligible, 18+, warning-labelled. Separately: **"moments leading up to
a serious accident or injury, even if the injury itself isn't shown"** is
18+/FYF-ineligible. **That is the near-miss format, named.** FYF-ineligible
videos remain findable via search and profile, and the flag is visible in
analytics.

*Verification note: TikTok's Sept 2025 policy rewrite moved the granular driving
bullets behind client-side expanders that could not be opened during research;
treat pre-2025 driving-specific language as directional. Meta's
recommendation-limitation claims rest on secondary reporting plus Community
Standards text, not a primary Meta page.*

## The legal layer — flag all of these to Sam

The source research missed this layer entirely. It was added by the adversarial
pass.

1. **Defamation.** The "I got scammed by [named dealership]" format is only
   available to someone with the paperwork. **No named business without
   documentation.** Frame as opinion, not factual assertion, wherever
   documentation is thin.
2. **Recording consent.** Two-party-consent states include **FL, CA, IL, PA, WA,
   MA, MD.** No identifiable third-party audio without consent. The marquee
   "cold-open on a third party's voice" example in the reference corpus was
   filmed in Florida.
3. **Customer PII in shop content.** Plates, VINs, repair-order names, faces,
   addresses. Every garage-diagnosis archetype puts someone's vehicle on camera.
4. **Disclosure is law, not tone.** FTC 16 CFR 255 (US) and ASA/CAP (UK): a
   loaned press vehicle, a comped part, or a covered trip is a **material
   connection requiring disclosure.**
5. **Press embargoes.** The first-look walkaround archetype runs on press-fleet
   access. Breaking an embargo means permanent OEM blacklisting — an existential
   risk to that whole format.
6. **Third-party footage rights.** A named licence source in the script metadata
   — ViralHog / Newsflare / Jukin licence ID, or a written grant that explicitly
   covers YouTube, TikTok **and** Instagram publication. Not Reddit rips, not
   "found on X." Submitter permission does not automatically cover platform
   publication; that is exactly what cost one 604K channel its appeals.
7. **Music.** Default to original engine/ambient audio or a licensed library
   track. **TikTok's catalogue licence does not travel to Meta or YouTube.**
   Business accounts are confined to the Commercial Music Library and Meta Sound
   Collection; any branded-content tag voids trending-audio use. On Shorts,
   licensed music shrinks the Creator Pool (1 track ≈ 50% to licensing, 2 ≈
   67%). **Incidental music at a cars-and-coffee or a show routinely triggers
   Rights Manager and Content ID mutes** — "engine noise is the answer" does not
   cover ambient capture at events.
8. **"Made for kids" misflagging.** Bright vehicles, "cars" keywords and
   toy-adjacent thumbnails draw MFK flags, which kill comments and personalised
   ads.
9. **Reused content from Sam's own long-form.** A mechanical re-cut is treated
   differently from an original upload. A Short derived from long-form needs
   original narration, an original edit thesis, or new framing — not just a
   trim.
10. **Helmet-cam jurisdiction.** The UK permits bracket-mounted helmet cams;
    France effectively bars public posting of helmet-cam footage; Austria and
    Portugal are stricter. Flag in the script brief.
11. **No cross-platform watermarks.** Produce clean masters and re-caption per
    platform. Watermarks are a named Instagram demotion and a TikTok
    unoriginality signal [both T1].

## The compliant crash format

Archetype K in `archetypes.md` is the one safe way to build on incident footage:

- Educational context in the **spoken audio and on-screen markup**, not a
  hashtag.
- **Cut before impact and before injury.**
- **Credit the clip source on screen** — solves rights and the "he stole this"
  attack in one move.
- Licence documented per item 6 above.
