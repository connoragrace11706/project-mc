# Format archetypes

Each entry: **cold open → tension → payoff → runtime → who can run it.**

Evidence tags: `[V]` re-verified against live channel data sorted by views,
31 Jul 2026. `[O]` single-pass observation — directionally useful, not a
performance profile. `[U]` untested; never present to the user as a finding.

## The corpus-level correction

**Instruction is the dominant winning format in automotive short-form.** An
early draft of this research claimed how-to framing underperforms; that came
from sampling recent-uploads windows and is backwards. Top Shorts on the exact
channels sampled: "Quick Lesson About The Clutch" 6.3M, "How To Swerve On A
Motorcycle" 2.5M, "How to Remove Locking Lug-Bolts without the Key" 6M, "How To
Make Your Toyota Last Over 300,000 Miles Part 1" 1.4M, "How To Ask A Car Dealer
To Lower Their Fees" 1.3M. `[V]`

What actually fails is the **templated multi-clause course-catalogue title** —
"Mastering Motorcycle Braking: Achieve Balance and Control in Your Stops." The
same channel's best performer is "How much lean angle before you lose traction
on your motorcycle?" — instruction phrased as a physical question with an
answer. `[V]`

This also aligns with the share research: **practical value beat anxiety** in
the field data (`craft/share-psychology.md`). Useful outperforms emotional.

## A. Macro-object cold open `[O, strong]`

**Open:** a single failed part fills the frame on a fingertip. Shallow DOF,
natural light, no music, no text. **Tension:** what did this do, and how did it
get like that. **Payoff:** name it deadpan at ~0.9s, then the mechanism.
**20–35s. Hands-only — which is the point: hands are the cheapest credential and
they defeat every gatekeeping attack.**

1.2M views for a photograph of a lug nut. Fastest measured opener in the corpus
at 0.87s. **Write shot one as "macro on [specific part], shallow DOF" before
writing anything wider.** One object beats one scene.

## B. Obscure-part explainer `[V — cleanest single result in the corpus]`

**Open:** the part in the hand, named, with a price. **Tension:** you have never
seen this and it solves a problem you have. **Payoff:** mechanism at 5–8s,
relevance to a normal vehicle at the end. **30–45s. On-camera or hands-only.**

A Formula Drift team's remote-oil-filter-adapter explainer did **430K against
22K for the next-best Short on the same channel.** An obscure component
explained by someone who actually uses it beat the spectacle the channel exists
for by roughly 20x.

## C. Short-imperative or answerable-question instruction `[V]`

**Open:** the task named in ≤6 words, or a physical question with a real answer.
**Tension:** you are doing this wrong, or you don't know the number. **Payoff:**
demonstration begins by 3–5s. **25–60s. On-camera or hands-only; faceless only
with original footage of the procedure.**

**If the script says "I torqued it to spec," the shot must include the wrench
clicking.**

## D. Quoted-claim disproof `[O, strong]`

**Open:** a scare-quoted third-party assertion as title and overlay — *"Other
Shop" Says You Need A Motor!*, *Customer States: Mount and Balance Tires*.
**Tension:** someone is wrong and you have the vehicle. **Payoff:** rebuttal
evidence on screen inside 4s. **30–50s.** The repair-order voice is unfakeable
if you don't work in a shop — gate on that.

## E. Walking evidence reveal `[O, strong]`

**Open:** unstabilised handheld vertical, VO poses a shared grievance as a
question. **Tension:** ≤2s gap between question and proof. **Payoff:** the walk
lands on physical proof. **30–45s. Own footage only.**

Measured: question at 2.07s, transmission on the concrete by 3.7s, 5.2M views.
**A rhetorical question with a delayed payoff is the clickbait pattern that gets
punished. Two seconds, maximum.**

## F. Failure post-mortem `[O, three independent channels]`

**Open:** the carnage on the bench, not the finished build. **Tension:** what
killed it. **Payoff:** the actual cause by 6–10s, the fix after. **30–60s.**

**Lead with the failure, not the win.** Admitting your own failure is the
highest-trust move available in this niche.

## G. Receipts / real cost `[O]`

**Open:** the number that hurts, in the first 3s. **Payoff:** itemisation —
"$4,200, and $1,800 of that was one sensor." **25–40s. Requires Sam's own
money.**

Hard rule: **never quote MSRP as if it were a purchase price.** Enthusiast
readers name that specific move as a trope they hate. Attach a qualifier:
"$22,000 **in 2004 money**."

## H. Roll-on pull with a spec title `[V]`

**Open:** named bike/car, stated speed band, engine. No hook, no jeopardy, no
personality. **Tension:** none needed — the sound and the number *are* the
product. **Payoff:** the pull, 3–8s in. **15–30s. Faceless-native. Infinitely
repeatable series scaffold.**

623K and 390K on a channel whose Shorts had been written off as failed. One of
the most repeatable formats in moto short-form.

## I. Silent spec card `[O, strong]`

**Open:** a pinned 2–3 line text card, top third, high contrast on a solid bar,
static through the entire opening. **No narration at all.** **Tension:** the
card states a complete claim; the sound is the reward for unmuting.
**Payoff event: 4–8s. 15–30s. Faceless-native.**

Multiple 500K–1.4M Shorts on one channel with **no auto-caption track at all**
— zero speech. Its 7.69M Short contains four spoken words in fourteen seconds:
*"3, 2, 1, go."*

Deliverable is **the card copy plus the sound event**, nothing else.

## J. Pure engine-note clip `[O]`

**Open:** bonnet-cam or side-tracking accel run, two-word label naming the exact
car, cut to the tach ~3s. No speech, no music. **10–20s. Faceless-native. Zero
claims means zero attack surface** — good low-risk cadence filler. Only works on
a vehicle with a known sonic reputation.

## K. Clip-as-evidence breakdown — the compliant crash format `[V for scale]`

**Open:** the incident runs once at speed. **Tension:** what was available to
the rider and when. **Payoff:** slowed replay with the read, on-screen markup.
**30–60s. The strongest faceless model, because the narrator owns the read, not
the footage.**

**Structural non-negotiables:** the educational context must live in the spoken
audio and the on-screen markup, because YouTube requires that context to "appear
in the images or audio of the video itself" [T1]. A safety hashtag under a shock
clip is worth nothing. **Cut before impact and before injury.** Credit the clip
source on screen — it solves rights and the "he stole this" attack at once. See
`risk.md` for licensing.

## L. Judgment invitation `[V]` — **gated**

**Open:** footage of someone making a mistake. **Tension:** the audience gets to
rule. **Payoff:** freeze on the error at 2–4s, your read at 4–15s. **25–40s.**

~1.1M each on two examples. This format delegates the gatekeeping impulse to the
audience instead of triggering it against you — **but only if the creator passes
the same test.** Gate on Sam's own on-camera gear and visible competence, not on
the topic. **A creator filmed in a hoodie cannot run this format at all**; it
inverts into a hypocrisy pile-on.

## M. Contrarian claim about a named object `[O, strong]`

**Open:** a specific, disputable assertion about a specific product, stated
flat. **Payoff:** the evidence, immediately. **30–50s.**

**Never pose the fight as an open question and walk away.** Take a side, give
the strongest version of the opposing case, explain the tiebreak. Note the
reference channel's register is sportbike/ADV-coded — **not portable to a bagger
audience.**

## N. Year/trim-range targeting explainer `[O]`

**Open:** "If you have a 2012–2014 Camry or a 2015 to 2017 Camry…" **Tension:**
you are being addressed personally. **30–50s.** Hyper-specific ranges make
owners feel named and create a legitimate correction surface that drives
comments. Reference channel Shorts run 1.2M–2.8M.

## O. Revive and drive `[O]`

Three-part frame: **duration of neglect + disqualifying obstacle + implied "can
we anyway."** *"Sitting in a carport for 6 years… doesn't have a battery… we
can."* **Payoff:** it runs, or it moves. **30–60s.**

## P. Letterboxed in-group ritual `[O]`

**Open:** 16:9 centred in 9:16 with black bars, text in the top bar formatted
"How to [ritual only owners of X do]." **The joke is the whole asset. 10–20s.**
7.7M on the reference example. **Deliberately unpolished — do not gate the hook
on production polish.** A 5.2M-view Short in this corpus is unstabilised
handheld phone footage.

⚠️ Letterboxing is a **named Instagram demotion** [T1]. This archetype is
YouTube/TikTok only unless reframed full-bleed for Reels.

## Q. EV-native archetypes

Every sound-led ceiling format above assumes an ICE soundtrack. EVs remove the
payload. Substitute:

- **Instrumented run.** Screen or gauge as hero shot: charge curve 10–80%,
  kWh/100mi at a stated speed and temperature, taper onset. The number replaces
  the noise. Title carries conditions: "0°C, 150kW charger, LFP pack."
- **Cost-per-mile receipts.** Archetype G with the electricity bill.
- **Physical teardown.** Pack module, rotor, contactor, busbar. Archetype B
  works identically and is arguably richer — almost nobody has seen these parts
  in hand.
- **Software-behaviour gripe.** A specific, reproducible OTA or UI failure,
  filmed. High comment density, no policy surface.
- **The silence as the subject.** Tire roar, wind noise at 70, one-pedal
  modulation, HVAC whine. Invert the sound rule: mic what you *can* hear.
- **Mechanism explainer.** Functionally faceless at the hook level because the
  authority is physics, checkable by anyone. Reference channel top Shorts run
  41M/23M/23M/22M/20M `[V]`.

**Never write EV content as a "soul" polemic from either side.** That argument is
identity, not spreadsheets — it produces jokes, not retention.

## Formats to avoid

| Format | Why |
|---|---|
| **Guess-the-car / guess-the-logo** | Template recognised on sight and swiped. One creator: 2,554 views on a guess hook against 67,400 on a value-buying hook `[O, n=1]`. Viable **only** as a real puzzle (partial taillight, ambiguous trim) or a second-order one ("real trim, replica, conversion, or upbadge?") |
| **AI-TTS narration over stock or scraped footage** | YouTube's inauthentic-content policy makes image slideshows, templated storylines and scrolling-text formats ineligible for monetisation; TikTok flags compilations and light re-uploads as unoriginal. **Faceless is fine. Templated-with-no-original-input is not.** |
| **Crash compilation aggregation** | Largest raw audience in moto, but it is an aggregation business, not a creator business, and the category most exposed to reused-content enforcement plus licensing claims. One 604K channel nearly lost everything to viral-licensing firms that had signed its own submitters; appeals failed because submitter permission didn't cover platform publication |
| **Withheld-payoff serialisation** | Nuance, not a ban. Serialisation works when each part restates the goal and delivers a complete unit (1.4M / 1.1M verified). What fails is Pt.1 ending *before* its payoff to force Pt.2 |
| **Stand-up wheelies, hands-free riding** | Tribal liability (reads as squid to ADV/cruiser/touring) **and** a monetisation liability. Seated wheelie is green; stand-up is yellow. See `risk.md` |
| **Copying a signature prop** | A recognisable prop is read as that creator's. Steal the *principle* — use a prop to demonstrate an error you cannot safely film — not the prop |
| **Staged discovery** | Filming before the drain plug comes out, multiple angles of an "unfolding" accident, implausibly clean fluids. Mechanically obvious and cheap to avoid |

## The equity gate

Wordless aesthetic B-roll and caption-only meme gags are **persona-dependent
formats.** A 9.7% like rate on a wordless clip is a function of audience
recognition, not of the shot. **On a cold account, the same footage needs a text
card that states a claim, and the same caption is just a stale meme.** Check
`voice/profile.md` for current audience equity before offering these.
