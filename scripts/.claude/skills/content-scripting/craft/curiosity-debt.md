# Curiosity Debt

Every gap you open is a debt. The payoff must beat a baseline the gap itself
raised, because **satisfying curiosity's default outcome is disappointment**
(see `attention.md`). Debt is a resource to be tracked, not a lever to pull
repeatedly.

## The schedule

Before writing the body, lay out an explicit schedule:

| Time | Gap opened | Gap paid | Debt outstanding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | What is this part / what broke | — | 1 |
| 5s | — | Named, mechanism started | 0 |
| 6s | Why it matters to *your* vehicle | — | 1 |
| 20s | — | The specific failure mode | 0 |

**Rule: never let outstanding debt exceed what has recently been paid.** Two
unpaid gaps at once is where a viewer disengages, because neither feels close to
resolution.

Write the schedule before the words. If you cannot fill the "gap paid" column,
you do not have a script — you have a tease.

## Platform-specific payoff placement

This is where the platforms genuinely diverge, and it is the single biggest
reason a TikTok cut fails as a Reel.

| Platform | Placement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** (60–90s for monetisation) | **3–4 chained micro-payoffs**, each ~20s block re-earning the next | Creator Rewards requires over 60s [T1]; retention requires the piece never coasts |
| **TikTok** (under 60s) | Single arc, payoff before the last quarter | No monetisation constraint, so no reason to stretch |
| **Reels** (8–15s) | **Front-load the payoff**, let the rest add texture | Reshare and rewatch both require the viewer to *already* feel rewarded. A punchline-at-the-end Reel is structurally wrong. |
| **Shorts** | **Open loop in sentence 1, closed in the last** | Average % viewed is a named ranking input [T1]; the viewer needs a stated reason to reach the end |

Reels and Shorts have **inverted** payoff placement. Treating them as one format
saves nothing and costs both.

## Series and part-2 content

The Ovsiankina resumption effect (~67% voluntary return across 21 publications,
T3) is the real mechanism, and it has a condition: **the interrupted unit has to
have been a unit.**

- **Works:** each part restates the goal in one clause and delivers a complete
  payoff of its own. Verified examples in this niche run 1.4M and 1.1M views on
  parts 1 and 3 of the same series.
- **Fails:** Part 1 that ends *before* its payoff in order to force Part 2. That
  is not an interruption, it is a withheld transaction, and it reads as one.

There is no blanket ban on serialisation. There is a ban on unpaid parts.

## Length

Pick the shortest length at which the payoff lands, then cut 15% of connective
tissue. Length is a commitment, not a free parameter — on Reels, watch time is
judged as a **percentile against reels of your own length** [T1], so a longer
piece must beat a harder curve.

If a hard constraint forces a cut (a monetisation floor, a character limit),
follow the platform file's declared **degradation order** rather than
improvising. Improvised cuts remove payoffs, which is exactly backwards.
