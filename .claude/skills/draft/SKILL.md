# Draft a Post

## Before writing

1. Read `brand/profile.md` for voice and the car's current state.
2. Read `playbook/what-works.md` — reuse structures that are proven, don't
   reinvent.
3. Read `playbook/voice-notes.md` — this is the accumulated record of how
   Connor rewrites my drafts. Weight it heavily.
4. If the post references a specific part, cost, or repair I don't have
   details on, **ask rather than invent**. Fabricated specifics are the fastest
   way to lose a technical audience's trust, and they're unrecoverable.

## Per-platform output

**YouTube (long-form)**
- 3 title options, under 60 chars, no clickbait the video won't pay off
- Thumbnail concept — describe the single readable-at-thumbnail-size image
- First 15 seconds scripted word for word. This is the whole ballgame; the
  rest can be looser.
- Beat-by-beat outline with rough timestamps
- Description with the first 2 lines carrying the hook
- Pinned comment suggestion (drives early engagement)

**Shorts / Reels / TikTok**
- Hook line — spoken in the first 2 seconds, on-screen text in the first frame
- 15–45s script with shot notes
- Caption, front-loaded
- The single loop or payoff moment
- 3–5 hashtags, mixed broad and niche. Hashtag volume is not a growth lever;
  don't pad.

**Instagram feed**
- Carousel structure, or a single-image concept
- Caption with a hook line, body, and a soft CTA
- Alt text

**X**
- The post, under 280
- A thread outline only if the idea genuinely needs one

## Voice checklist

Before handing it over, check the draft against these:

- [ ] Would Connor actually say this out loud? Read it aloud mentally.
- [ ] Is there a real number, part name, or specific detail — not vague hype?
- [ ] Does it admit difficulty or cost where relevant? That's the trust engine.
- [ ] Is it legible to someone who has never turned a wrench?
- [ ] Does the hook promise something the post actually delivers?
- [ ] Zero car-YouTuber hype voice. No "you won't BELIEVE."

## After

Ask Connor to paste back the version he actually posted. Log meaningful
rewrites in `playbook/voice-notes.md` with the before/after. That file is the
mechanism by which my drafts stop needing rewrites.
