## One-line positioning

**Building a 500whp time-attack E36 M3 in a home garage with no lift — and
publishing every real number, including the ones that make me look stupid.**

### Why this replaced the old line

The original positioning was *"racing on a working person's budget."* The
receipts don't support it: $25k for the car in 2020, $40k+ in swap parts,
**~$65k all-in.** An audience that is promised "budget" and shown $65k stops
trusting everything else on the channel — and trust is the only asset here.

The real differentiator was never that it's cheap. It's:

- **No shop labor.** Every hour is Connor's, in a home garage, **with no lift.**
- **No blank check.** A full-time job paid for this over five years.
- **No hiding the number.** Almost nobody in this space publishes real costs.
  The competitor isn't a guy with more money — it's the channel with a silent
  sponsor covering the engine.

So the hook is honesty and self-reliance, not frugality. The Money pillar gets
*stronger* under this framing: "why I spent $40k instead of $10k rebuilding the
S50" beats "how I saved $200."

**Design implication:** the failure mode for two-person build channels is
coordination, not effort — he's wrenching and can't direct, she's shooting and
doesn't know which shot carries the story. Fix it *before* the camera turns on:
title and thumbnail decided first, shot list derived from it.

**On G's channel:** it's a spin-off focused on her **photography business**, not
a second car channel. Strategy is deliberately different — photographers sell on
**Instagram first, then Pinterest**; YouTube is a distant third. Launch it off
the back of the main channel's audience rather than cold. The two feed each
other: the build supplies portfolio work, the main channel showcases her
shooting, her channel converts that into clients.

## Who this is for

**Primary:** 18–34, car-enthusiast-adjacent. They own a car they care about,
watch build content, and want to believe they could do it too.

**Secondary:** Grassroots racers — HPDE, autocross, time attack, club racing.
Smaller, far higher engagement, and where credibility and sponsors come from.

**Third (via G):** photography clients and photographers — a different audience
on different platforms, served by the spin-off.

**Not for:** supercar/exotic audiences. Different economics, different values.

### The bottom end — this is the channel's spine

**The bottom end is stock.** Supporting mods only:

- Upgraded oil pump
- Cams, timing gear and chain
- Upgraded valve springs and retainers
- ARP head studs
- Possibly polished pistons

**Stated policy, in his words: *"We are not building it until we blow it up."***

This is the single strongest content asset in the project:

1. It's a **defensible budget engineering call** — stock K24 internals take a
   lot, and pre-emptively building it is $8–10k not spent today.
2. A large number of people will insist it's insane, loudly, in the comments.
3. It creates a **series arc with a built-in cliffhanger nobody had to fake.**
   Every video after the first dyno pull carries one unspoken question: *does it
   hold?*

Say the policy on camera, early, in exactly those words.

## Voice

- First person, plain language, no car-YouTuber hype voice.
- **Admit mistakes on camera.** The failed weld, the wrong part, the DNF.
  Competence plus fallibility is the whole trust equation.
- Explain the **why**, not just the what. A part swap is boring; a part swap
  where you explain the failure mode is a lesson.
- Never talk down. Assume the viewer is smart but new.

### Profanity and smoking — decided 2026-07-31

**Connor's position:** he curses a lot, has been cutting back on camera, smokes
cigarettes, and **wants to stay true to himself.**

**Ruling: stay true to himself.** For a brand whose entire value is "this is a
real guy telling you the truth," a sanitized on-camera persona costs more than
the ad revenue it protects. A guy visibly performing a cleaner version of
himself is exactly the thing this channel exists to be the opposite of.

The cost is real but manageable, and it's mostly avoidable with placement rather
than censorship:

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Keep the first 7 seconds clean** | This is the window YouTube weighs most heavily for ad eligibility. It's also the hook — you want it tight and quotable anyway. Costs nothing. |
| **Keep titles and thumbnails clean** | Profanity there is the most reliable way to limit ads, and it doesn't help CTR in this niche. |
| **Curse naturally in the body** | Guidelines relaxed considerably in 2023; moderate profanity through the body of a video is broadly fine now. |
| **Smoking: incidental, never the subject** | Tobacco *depiction* is treated far more leniently than tobacco *focus*. If a cig is in your hand while you're explaining a head gasket, fine. Don't do a bit about smoking. |
| **Expect TikTok to be stricter** | TikTok restricts tobacco depiction harder than YouTube and may suppress reach. If a clip is tobacco-visible, favour YouTube/IG for it. |

> ⚠ **Unverified.** The specific thresholds above are from my general knowledge
> and I was blocked from confirming them against YouTube's current
> advertiser-friendly guidelines. **Verify before relying on them for a
> monetization decision.**
