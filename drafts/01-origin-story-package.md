## Title options

Ranked. Each has to carry three things: the failure, the money, and the
controversy — because those are the three the audience can't get anywhere else.

### A. "My M3's Engine Blew. So I Spent $40,000 on a Honda." ← recommended
Failure, a real number, and the Honda heresy in eleven words. The curiosity gap
is doing real work: *why would blowing a BMW engine lead to a Honda one, and how
does that cost $40k?* You have to watch to resolve it.

### B. "$40,000 to Put a Honda Motor in a BMW M3"
Money-forward, tests **H1** most directly. Slightly flatter — states the
situation instead of opening a question.

### C. "I Spent $40,000 Making My M3 Worse"
Strongest pure curiosity gap, weakest on information. Risks feeling like bait,
which is a hard-line violation if the video doesn't pay it off. It *can* pay off
— to a purist it genuinely is worse — but it's the riskiest of the three.

### D. "Why I Put a Honda K24 in My E36 M3"
Safest, most searchable, lowest ceiling. The one everyone else would pick.

**Avoid:** anything resembling the current pattern. *"Ifykyk"*, *"M3!!! 🤣🤣"*,
emoji-only titles, and hashtag walls. The baseline is explicit — the 33-hashtag
post has the **lowest like rate on the account at 1.2%**, roughly 5× worse than
posts with a handful. Distribution is already being handed to you; the loss is
entirely at packaging.

## Thumbnail concept

**The two engines, side by side.** Blown S50 on the left — visibly dead, oil,
damage showing. K24 on the right, clean, on the stand. Your hands or your face
in frame for scale and humanity.

- **Text overlay: `$40,000`.** One element. Nothing else.
- No arrows, no red circles, no shocked face. The brand is "this guy tells the
  truth" — thumbnail conventions that scream *content* undercut that.
- If the blown motor is already gone, the fallback is you holding the failed
  part with the K24 behind you.

**Shoot this before anything else.** Title and thumbnail come first, and the rest
of the video is built to pay them off.

## Structure

Target 12–18 minutes. Timestamps are intent, not a stopwatch.

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:07** | The blown motor, in hand. State the number. **Clean — no profanity in the ad-eligibility window.** |
| 0:07–2:00 | What the car was. Bought 2020, $25k, base M3, Confetti Vaders. What you did to it. What it sounded like. |
| 2:00–4:00 | **The failure.** What let go, how it felt, what it looked like when it came apart. Do not soften this. |
| 4:00–7:00 | **The decision.** Rebuild the S50 (~$10k, back to stock power) vs. LS vs. 1JZ vs. K24. Why each one lost. **Real numbers on screen for each option.** |
| 7:00–11:00 | **What $40k actually buys.** Itemised. This is the segment nobody else in this niche will make. |
| 11:00–14:00 | Where it is now. Subframe done, drivetrain next, parts arriving. Garage, no lift, both of you. |
| 14:00–end | **The stock bottom end.** *"We're not building it until we blow it up."* Your words. Then the question that carries the series: **does it hold?** |

## Description + metadata

- Open with the cost breakdown as text — it's the searchable, screenshot-able,
  shareable asset.
- Chapters matching the beats above.
- **3–5 tags. Not 33.** `bmw e36 m3`, `k24 swap`, `engine swap cost`,
  `time attack build`.
- Pinned comment: the question. *"Stock bottom end, 500whp target. How long does
  it last? I'll post the answer either way."*
- **Check every frame for employer identifiers** — badge, uniform, work vehicle,
  branded anything. Hard line, and this is old footage shot before it was a rule.
